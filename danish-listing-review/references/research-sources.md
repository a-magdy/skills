# Research sources

Danish public data is unusually open, which means most of what a buyer needs is findable —
the difficulty is knowing which source answers which question. Web-search the specifics;
this file tells you where to point.

Always cite URLs. The user may show this to a partner, an adviser or the agent, and an
unsourced claim about crime rates or crash history collapses at the first challenge.

## Property, registers and planning

| Source | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **ois.dk** / **boligejer.dk** | Official property data, BBR, valuations, planning status |
| **bbr.dk** | Registered areas, use, build and renovation years. Compare against reality — discrepancies transfer to the buyer |
| **tinglysning.dk** | Encumbrances, charges, servitutter |
| **vurderingsportalen.dk** / **skat.dk** | Public assessment, tax basis, whether provisional, current rates |
| **planinfo.dk**, **plandata.dk** | Kommuneplan and lokalplan — the binding restrictions |
| Kommune website | Local plans in progress, construction projects, school districts, rates |

**Read the lokalplan properly when the property needs exterior work.** If it mandates uniform
roof material or facade treatment across a bebyggelse, that dictates both the cost and the
options for a roof replacement — and it is the kind of constraint that turns a 250,000 kr job
into a 400,000 kr one. The salgsopstilling names the applicable plans; look them up.

## Prices, history and market position

| Source | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **boliga.dk** | Sold prices, price history per address, days on market, price cuts, area statistics |
| **dinggeo.dk**, **boligsiden.dk** | Comparable sales, area trends |
| **Danmarks Statistik (dst.dk)**, tables `EJEN`/`BM011` | Official price indices by region and property type |
| **Finans Danmark** housing statistics | Quarterly price development, regional breakdown |

### The 2008 crash — what to actually look up

Denmark's crash was severe and regionally uneven. Central Copenhagen fell hard but recovered
within a few years; **commuter-belt suburbs and provincial towns generally fell further and
took substantially longer** — in some areas nominal recovery took until the late 2010s.

For the specific postcode, establish:

- the 2006–2007 peak price per m² for this property type
- the trough, and the year it occurred (usually 2009–2013 depending on area)
- **peak-to-trough percentage**
- how many years to regain the nominal peak
- the same three figures for Copenhagen city and the national average, for comparison

This is the best available proxy for downside risk, and it is the thing buyers most
consistently fail to check. Present it as: *"in a comparable downturn, this postcode
historically fell X% and took Y years to recover"* — concrete, sourced, and directly relevant
to the break-even calculation.

### Market position = negotiating leverage

- **Liggetid** — how long has it been listed? Boliga shows the full listing history.
- **Price cuts** — has it already been reduced, by how much, and when?
- **Competing supply** — how many comparable units in the same bebyggelse or street are
  listed simultaneously?
- **Salgspris vs. udbudspris ratio** for the postcode and property type — what do places here
  actually sell for versus ask?
- **Average liggetid** locally, as a measure of seller time pressure

A property that has sat five months with one reduction is a fundamentally different
negotiation from one listed last week. This section often does more for the user than the
entire technical analysis.

## Crime and safety

| Source | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **politi.dk** district statistics | Reported crime by police district |
| **Danmarks Statistik** `STRAF`-series | Offences by municipality, type, over time |
| **Trygfonden / Tryghedsmålingen** | Perceived safety surveys by area |
| Ministry's **parallelsamfund / udsatte boligområder** list | Whether any designated area is nearby |

Benchmark against both the national and the regional average, and give rates per capita
rather than raw counts — a large municipality always has more incidents in absolute terms.

**Municipal averages routinely mislead in Denmark.** A kommune can have an elevated overall
figure driven by one concentrated area while the specific residential enclave is entirely
ordinary. Where possible, distinguish the neighbourhood from the municipal aggregate, and say
plainly when you can only find municipal-level data.

## Demographics and facilities

| Source | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **Danmarks Statistik / statistikbanken.dk** | Age, household composition, income, education, employment by municipality and sogn |
| **noegletal.dk** | Municipal key figures, comparable across kommuner |
| **uddannelsesstatistik.dk** | School data and results |
| **Google Maps / OSM** | Distances and walking times to shops, schools, station, green space |
| **Rejseplanen.dk** | Real transit journey times and transfer counts |
| **Krak / degulesider** | Local amenities |

For commute, use Rejseplanen for transit (capture **both** minutes and transfer count) and
realistic road or cycle routing for the other modes. Evaluate every configured mode for every
destination — one workable option is what matters, not the average.

## Environment and risk

| Source | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **miljoeportalen.dk** (Danmarks Miljøportal) | Soil contamination, områdeklassificering, drinking-water interests |
| **klimatilpasning.dk**, kommune flood maps | Flood and groundwater risk |
| **Miljøstyrelsen noise maps** | Road, rail and aircraft noise exposure |
| **radonfrithjem.dk**, kommune radon data | Local radon risk — relevant for slab-on-grade houses |
| **Energistyrelsen** | District heating tariffs and trajectory |

`Områdeklassificeret — lettere forurenet` appears on essentially every urban Danish property
and is routine, not alarming. Say so rather than flagging it as a finding — over-flagging
routine items costs you credibility on the items that actually matter.

## Associations

For rowhouses, terraced houses and flats, the grundejerforening or ejerforening materially
affects cost and risk. Request from the agent or the association: current accounts, budget,
latest general-meeting minutes, planned or approved assessments, and current debt.

Two things to look for specifically:

- **Pending collective works.** An approved roof or facade project means a per-member bill
  that may not appear anywhere in the salgsopstilling.
- **Debt listed as `afventer`.** The salgsopstilling sometimes shows association debt as
  pending/unresolved at listing date. That is an open liability, not an absence of one.

Also note the post-2024 property tax reform exposure: associations owning common land may
face grundskyld reassessment and back-adjustment, flowing through to member contributions.
Several salgsopstillinger now carry boilerplate about this; it is a real cost risk, not just
legal throat-clearing.

## Honesty about gaps

Some of this will not be findable at neighbourhood granularity — Danish statistics are often
published at municipal or sogn level only. When that happens, say which level you found and
that finer data was unavailable. Do not silently present a municipal figure as if it
described the street.

# Fieldwork — what only the buyer can check

Document analysis has a hard ceiling. Nobody can tell from a PDF whether the street feels
safe at eleven on a Saturday night, whether there is anywhere to park after seven, or what
the neighbour who has lived there twenty years knows about the roof.

This section closes that gap. Adapt it to the property — **do not paste it wholesale.** A
ground-floor flat and a detached house need different lists, and a report padded with
irrelevant instructions gets skimmed instead of used. Pick what applies, phrase it for this
specific place, and drop the rest.

The quality bar: every item should be something a person can physically go and do, with a
clear signal to look for. "Check for damp" is useless. "Smell each room, especially bathrooms
and any corner against an outside wall — damp has a smell before it has a stain" is
actionable.

## Before the first viewing

- Walk or cycle the actual commute route at the real time you would travel it. Routing
  estimates omit the level crossing, the hill and the unlit stretch.
- Drive or walk past unannounced, without the agent present.

## At the viewing — things to do, not look at

Agents expect passive viewers. Doing physical checks is normal and reasonable; the ones that
find problems are almost all things that take under a minute.

- Run every tap at once. Check pressure, how long hot water takes to arrive, and how fast it
  recovers.
- Flush every toilet and watch the drainage speed. Slow drainage on original pipes is a
  signal worth a TV-inspection.
- Open and close every window and exterior door — sticking frames, failed catches, and
  condensation *between* panes (a punctured seal, not surface moisture).
- Go into the loft yourself. Insulation depth, daylight visible through the roof, staining on
  the underside of the boards.
- Smell each room, particularly bathrooms, utility rooms and corners against an outside wall.
- Press on bathroom floor and wall tiles, especially in the shower, and listen for hollowness.
- Check mobile signal in every room, including the basement if there is one.
- Photograph every wall, every ceiling junction, and the full roof from outside.
- **Look at the neighbours' roofs.** Have they already been replaced? On a terrace or an
  estate built in one go, that tells you the true remaining life of this one and whether a
  collective project — and a shared bill — is coming.
- Where relevant: check the boiler/heat unit age and service records, the consumer unit for
  an HPFI relay, and whether radiators heat evenly.

## Return visits — go at least three more times

A single midday viewing shows the property at its most flattering. The pattern that works:

- **Weekday morning, 07:30–08:30.** Traffic, school-run congestion, parking turnover.
- **Weekday evening, 19:00–21:00.** Count the available parking spaces — this is when it is
  worst. Check street lighting and how it feels walking from the station.
- **Friday or Saturday night, after 22:00.** Noise, gatherings, whether it feels safe.
- **Sunday daytime.** What the neighbourhood is actually like to live in.
- **In rain.** Standing water on the plot, overflowing gutters, damp patches appearing that
  were invisible when dry.
- **At different hours,** note where sun actually falls in the garden and the main rooms.

## People to talk to — go and knock

This is the highest-value hour available, and almost nobody does it.

- **Both immediate neighbours.** Essential for anything attached, where you share walls and
  often a roof. Ask: how long have you lived here, what has gone wrong with your house, what
  has the association done recently, what would you change, and — the one that gets the real
  answer — *is there anything you'd want to know if you were the one buying?*
- **The association chair.** Planned assessments, current debt, disputes, pending shared
  works. Ask in writing so you have a record.
- **Anyone walking a dog or pushing a pram.** They will tell you things the agent will not,
  and they have no stake in the sale.

## Verify yourself, with authorities and providers

- Broadband availability and actually achievable speed at the address, not the postcode.
- School catchment — confirm it, and visit the school.
- Parking rules: permits, guest parking, allocated versus free-for-all.
- Bin storage, collection arrangements, post and parcel delivery.
- If the property needs exterior work, confirm with the kommune what the lokalplan permits
  *before* you price the job.

## The question to end on

After the roof, the bathroom and the electrical work — whatever the specific findings were —
ask honestly:

> If all of it needed doing in year one, would I still want this house at this price?

That question does more work than any score. If the answer is yes, the defects are a
negotiating position. If it is no, no amount of analysis will fix it.
